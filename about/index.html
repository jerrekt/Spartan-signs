About this project

A smart LED sign of the SJSU Spartan.

Students in the Society of Manufacturing Engineers at San José State are building an LED sign of the Spartan helmet, plus the production process to make more of them. You switch a sign on and off, set its colors and schedule it from this site.

How it works

1The sign

The Spartan helmet logo lit by LEDs in three color zones: white, blue and gold. An ESP32-C6 inside each sign runs WLED, an open-source LED controller. It takes its commands from the control site.

2The control site

Sign in, then add a sign with the code on its sticker. Turn it on or off, set brightness, effects and zone colors, schedule a nightly on-and-fade-out, share it with other people, or open a support ticket. Each control maps to a WLED setting.

3The accounts

Anyone can sign in with Google or an email link, not only @sjsu.edu. There is one master user. Owners sit one level below (a person, a department or a club), and each owner can share their sign with others and remove them again.

How it is connected

Working todayPlanned, not built yet
Hosting
A container on Kratos, our home server. The public address reaches it through a secure tunnel, so the server is not open to the internet directly.
Updates
The code lives on GitHub. The container checks every minute and deploys anything new, so a change that is merged goes live within about a minute.
Signs
Each sign joins Wi-Fi, or Zigbee through a hub such as Home Assistant, and takes its commands from the site. Hardware control is not built yet.
Time
The clock syncs to pool.ntp.org in Pacific time, and schedules can follow sunset.
Help
Support tickets notify the project lead on Discord, and each sign can remove a user or reset its password.

Status

Prototyping. The Control and Lab pages are clickable demos with example signs and nothing talks to real hardware yet. Lab compares ten simulated build options for the face, wall and LEDs, so the team can choose a design before building.
